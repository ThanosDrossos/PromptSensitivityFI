## 1. Audit of the seminar folder's own PDF library

Five files in the project root were uncatalogued or mislabelled in the hand-off. Three of them matter.

### 1.1 ✅ FIRST-HAND — `37_Position_Uncertainty_Quanti.pdf` is **not** the paper the hand-off guessed

The hand-off (§2.2) speculated this was Chen/Da/Liu/Wei, *"Position: Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs
is Just Unsupervised Clustering"* (arXiv:2605.19220). **It is not.**

It is **Kirchhof, Kasneci & Kasneci, "Position: Uncertainty Quantification Needs Reassessment for
Large-language Model Agents", ICML 2025 (PMLR 267)**, University of Tübingen / TU Munich.

Why this matters more than the paper it was mistaken for: the abstract proposes three research
directions, and the **first one is named "underspecification uncertainties, for when users do not
provide all the information or define the exact task at the first go."**

That is this project's thesis, named as an open research direction at a top-tier venue, one year before
this work. The correct reading is **supporting, not scooping** — it is a position paper that argues the
aleatoric/epistemic dichotomy is inadequate for LLM agents and calls for exactly this construct to be
studied. It should be cited in the Introduction as the statement of the gap the project fills.

### 1.2 ✅ FIRST-HAND — `PromptEngineering___ECIR_25-3.pdf` is direct prior art on the probe contribution

**"Benchmarking Prompt Sensitivity in Large Language Models"**, ECIR 2025. This paper was in the folder,
unnamed, and appears nowhere in the hand-off, the review, or `references.bib`.

What it does, read directly:

- It **formally defines the task of *Prompt Sensitivity Prediction***: given a prompt `P_i`, predict
  whether the LLM will produce a response that satisfies the underlying information need `I_Pi`.
- It releases **PromptSET**: 11,469 prompts from TriviaQA and HotpotQA, each with 9 variations
  (~114,690 total), variations generated by **LLaMA-3.1-8B and Mistral-Nemo**, filtered for semantic
  similarity above a threshold, information-need preservation, and removal of hallucinated content,
  requiring at least 9 surviving variations per prompt.
- It benchmarks three baseline families: **LLM self-evaluation**, **text classification** (BERT), and
  **query performance prediction** (pre-retrieval QPP — the neural-embedding *specificity* metrics
  Closeness Centrality, Degree Centrality, Inverse Edge Frequency, PageRank, plus the supervised
  BERT-PE).
- Headline numbers (their Table 2, accuracy on PromptSET-TriviaQA / -HotpotQA, Mistral answers):
  BERT text classification **0.660 / 0.526**; supervised QPP BERT-PE **0.648 / 0.710**; the
  specificity-based QPP metrics **0.481–0.506 / 0.533–0.565** — at or near chance.
- Their conclusion is that existing methods do not adequately solve the task.

**Consequence for the project.** Three separate things follow, and they cut in different directions:

1. **The prompt-checker heads are not a new task.** Predicting from the question alone whether an LLM
   will answer it correctly is a named, benchmarked task with a public dataset. This must be cited, and
   the "reliability head" must be positioned as an entry in it.
2. **It answers hand-off open question #7 (QPP positioning) directly**, and better than a generic QPP
   survey would, because it applies QPP *to this exact task*. The project's OOD AUROC of 0.667 now has
   a natural comparison class rather than a vacuum. Note the units differ — they report accuracy and
   F1, the project reports AUROC — so the comparison must be made carefully, not by putting 0.667
   next to 0.660 as though they were the same statistic.
3. **It is prior art for the supervisor's x\* idea.** Their specificity-based QPP baselines come from
   the Arabzadeh/Zarrinkalam/Bagheri line — including *"Geometric estimation of specificity within
   embedding spaces"* (CIKM 2019) and neural embedding-based specificity metrics for pre-retrieval QPP
   (IP&M 57(4), 2020). Measuring **query specificity as embedding geometry** is an established IR
   subfield. That is structurally what `ESS_in` and the x\*-distance analysis do.
   Their finding that these metrics perform near chance on prompt variations — because "the specificity
   levels across the original prompts and their variations are too similar" — is a published precedent
   for the project's own null result that `ESS_in` is uncorrelated with everything.

### 1.3 ✅ FIRST-HAND — `Corona et al. …(Extende.pdf` already publishes the project's FI_in formula

**Corona, Di Benedetto, Gabriele, Giancarlo & Utro, "Functional Information, Biomolecular Messages and
Complexity of BioSequences and Structures (Extended Abstract)", Dagstuhl Seminar Proceedings 10231
(2010)**, `http://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2010/2688`.

Their §1.1 defines functional information as follows: let Σ be an alphabet and Σ\* the free monoid; fix
a **finite subset U of Σ\*** and a measurable function F over U; let `N_k` be the number of sequences in
U whose measure is at least k. Then **`FI(k) = −log₂(N_k / |U|)`**.

That is the project's `FI_in(q,k) = −log₂(N_k(q) / |U_q|)` — same formula, same symbols, sixteen years
earlier, defined over strings.

**This cuts both ways, and the second way is more important:**

- Against the project: the *formalisation* of FI over a finite string set is not new, and "we transfer
  Equation (Hazen) to prompts through three substitutions" describes a step already taken in print.
- **For the project:** the review's recommended fix (R5 — make FI generator-relative, `FI_in^G`, because
  `U_q` is 10 Phi-4 samples rather than Hazen's full configuration space) is **not an ad-hoc dodge to
  escape a criticism. It is the established formulation.** Corona et al. define U as a finite subset
  precisely to sidestep Hazen's "all possible configurations" requirement. Citing them converts the
  project's biggest formal vulnerability into a standard, defensible move.

This is the single most useful citation the audit turned up.

## 2. The FI provenance chain — verified first-hand, and worse for the novelty claim than recorded

### 2.1 ✅ FIRST-HAND — Szostak (2003), *Nature* 423:689

Read directly. The article headline is "Molecular messages"; "Functional information" is the title of
the concept box. Szostak defines functional information as **"−log₂ of the probability that a random
sequence will encode a molecule with greater than any given degree of function"**, gives the
ATP-binding aptamer example (~1 in 10¹¹ → **about 37 bits**, against 140 bits to specify a unique
70-mer), and states that functional information **"is not a property of any one molecule, but of the
ensemble of all possible sequences, ranked by activity."**

The project's talk track states all three of these correctly.

### 2.2 ✅ FIRST-HAND — Hazen et al. (2007), PNAS 104(suppl 1):8574–8581, §"The Functional Information of Letter Sequences"

I read pp. 8575–8576 directly. The review's finding is confirmed, and there is more in the passage than
the hand-off recorded.

Confirmed:
- Configuration space = sequences of *n* letters, N = 26ⁿ; `I(E_x) = −log₂[M(E_x)/26ⁿ]`.
- The degree of function is explicitly a **receiver-response probability**: E_x "might represent the
  probability that a local fire department will understand and respond to the message," described as
  "a measure (in this case from 0 to 1) of the effectiveness of the message in invoking a response."
- Worked example: 10-letter sequences with E_x ≈ 1 such as "FIREONMAIN", "MAINSTFIRE", "MAPLENMAIN";
  on the order of 1,000 such combinations ⇒ **I ≈ −log₂[1000/26¹⁰] ≈ 36 bits**.
- Degraded variants are discussed explicitly — phonetic misspellings, grammatical mistakes, typing
  errors — as yielding "a significant but lower probability of response."

**Additional material the hand-off missed, and it makes the collision tighter.** Hazen also runs a
*specificity* argument on the same example: sequences of 1–3 letters "are unlikely to convey sufficient
information to achieve any response"; with 4 letters a few suggestive configurations exist (FIRE, MAIN,
MAPL) but "all such sequences possess a high degree of ambiguity (i.e., E_x ≪ 1)"; with n ≫ 10 the
number of messages of a given degree of function increases dramatically. **Hazen therefore already
sketches the ambiguity → specificity → degree-of-function relationship that FI_spec operationalises**,
not merely FI-for-language.

**But the same passage supplies the project's escape route.** Hazen calls his own letter-sequence
treatment **qualitative** ("Although this conceptual example is qualitative, it introduces key concepts…"),
and states the requirement the project cannot meet: rigorous FI analysis "requires knowledge of two
attributes: (i) all possible configurations of the system … and (ii) the degree of function x for every
configuration," immediately adding that "these two requirements are difficult to meet in many systems."

So the defensible claim is not *first application* but **first quantitative instantiation with a real
receiver and a measured degree of function**, under an explicitly finite, generator-defined
configuration space in the sense of Corona et al. (§1.3).

### 3.2 The four confirmed scoops — real, fetched, and each takes something away

**① Zhang, Duan, Kim & Xu, "Sparse Neurons Carry Strong Signals of Question Ambiguity in LLMs",
arXiv:2509.13664 — EMNLP 2025 main conference. THREAT: HIGH.**
Trains probes on ambiguity-encoding neurons, **on AmbigQA — the project's own dataset** — using the
same label construction (original underspecified question = ambiguous, disambiguated variant = clear).
Transfers **AmbigQA → SituatedQA**: Mistral-7B 90.30→92.35 %, Llama-3.1-8B 88.60→94.00 %,
Gemma-7B 92.00→96.90 % accuracy. Also does causal steering.
**The vagueness head cannot be presented as novel.** Peer-reviewed, one year earlier, same dataset,
same construct, and it already has the cross-dataset transfer result the project treats as its
remaining probe novelty.

**② Kossen, Han, Razzak, Schut, Malik & Gal, "Semantic Entropy Probes", arXiv:2406.15927. THREAT: HIGH.**
Linear probes on hidden states approximating semantic entropy from a single generation.
**Correction to the review's proposed defence, which I checked against the code:** the review suggested
differentiating on token position, since SEP probes at TBG and SLT. But SEP's **TBG is "token before
generation", i.e. the last prompt token — exactly the position `scripts/dump_hidden_states.py` uses.**
The feature extraction is *identical*. The only surviving differentiators are the **target**
(SEP binarises semantic entropy via an Otsu-style threshold and fits logistic regression; note the
project's `train_fi_probes.py` uses **the same Otsu `gamma_star` trick**) and the *other* heads —
ambiguity and FI_in — which have no SEP counterpart. Lead with those, not with token position.

**③ Cencerrado et al., "No Answer Needed: Predicting LLM Answer Accuracy from Question-Only Linear
Probes", arXiv:2509.10625 — ICLR 2026 workshop poster. THREAT: HIGH.**
Activations taken **after the question is read but before any answer token**, linear probes predicting
whether the forthcoming answer is correct, transferring OOD across three model families 7B–70B, beating
verbalised confidence. **This is the reliability head.** The surviving differentiator is that they
predict one answer's correctness while the project predicts a *paraphrase-distribution* quantity
(FI_in, the surviving fraction) — that distinction must now become the explicit contribution.

**④ Polo et al., PromptEval, NeurIPS 2024. THREAT: HIGH.**
Already established, but the review under-stated it: PromptEval does not merely evaluate efficiently,
it **estimates the full performance distribution over prompts with proven consistency and reports
quantiles**. FI_in is the log of that distribution's survival function. The project cannot claim to
introduce the distribution-over-prompts view. The residual is **granularity and units** — PromptEval is
benchmark-level over prompt *templates*; FI_in is per-question over paraphrases of one question.

### 3.3 The two that were mis-verified in the hand-off but are more useful than claimed

**Żatuchin, arXiv:2607.13304 — real, and the hand-off was right to make it priority #1, but it does
not scoop ρ_F.** Read directly, pages 1–14: §4.1 fits a crossed random-effects model by REML
(statsmodels MixedLM) with three nested specifications, and **§4.2 is an explicit G-theory decision
study**. Table 3 gives within-prompt resampling = 34.8 % of variance, query language 32.0 %, model
1.7 %, brand 1.6 %, prompt main effect 0.3 %. Every component is reported as an ICC.
**Verdict: it publishes the apparatus first, so ρ_F cannot claim methodological novelty for the
resampling/paraphrase split.** But the estimand differs on every axis that matters — the outcome is a
**zero-inflated continuous sentiment score, not binary task success** (a binary refit is explicitly
deferred to future work in their §5.4); components are **corpus-level, not per-question**; the object
is a *brand*, not a question; paraphrase is a weak 3-level facet worth 0.3–1.1 %; and no confidence
intervals are computed. **ρ_F's estimand survives; its apparatus does not.**

**Pecher, Spiegel, Belanec & Cegin, arXiv:2602.04297 — real. THREAT: HIGH on framing, LOW on instruments.**
Full title: *"Revisiting Prompt Sensitivity in Large Language Models for Text Classification: The Role
of Prompt Underspecification"*. The abstract states verbatim that **"a significant portion of the
observed prompt sensitivity can be attributed to prompt underspecification"**, and their §5 is a Linear
Probe Analysis on per-layer activations.
**The project can no longer claim first publication of the underspecification thesis.** But they run at
**temperature 0 with no sampling**, so they structurally cannot separate phrasing variance from
decoding noise — no ICC, no variance components. Their underspecification is **instruction-side prompt
formatting**, not question-side semantic ambiguity. Their probes predict the **class label**, not
ambiguity, dispersion or reliability. And the domain is text classification, not open-domain QA.

### 3.4 The methodological hits — these attack the instruments, not the novelty

These are more dangerous than the scoops, because they cannot be answered by re-positioning.

- **McCabe et al., arXiv:2509.14478 (semantic alphabet size). HIGH.** Shows the plugin/observed
  estimator underestimates semantic entropy *because* the observed support misses unsampled classes,
  and runs its primary experiments **at n = 10 — the project's exact k**. Proposes a Good-Turing
  hybrid, `|S|^GT = kn/(n−f₁)`, plus a Chao–Shen-style correction, and shows the correction *improves*
  error detection. `metrics/fi_out.py:82-95` uses **plain observed richness**, with Chao1 removed in
  2026-06-29 for over-estimating. **That one-sentence dismissal is no longer sufficient** — but note
  the paper supplies the defence too: Chao1 is undefined with zero doubletons. The fix it points to is
  Good-Turing, not "leave it uncorrected". Corroborated independently by two further 2026 preprints
  (arXiv:2605.00668, arXiv:2604.19162) and by **Nguyen et al., Findings of ACL 2025 (arXiv:2506.00245)**
  on small-k cluster saturation, who also use k = 10.
- **Tomov et al., arXiv:2511.04418, "The Illusion of Certainty". HIGH.** Builds **AmbigQA\*** directly
  from Min et al. (2020) with ground-truth answer distributions, and evaluates semantic entropy *and*
  linear probes on residual-stream activations at the final pre-generation token. It occupies three of
  the project's pillars at once and is a **negative result**: UQ estimators are theoretically limited
  under genuine aleatoric ambiguity. The escapes are real — no paraphrases, no variance decomposition
  of task success, no use of the annotated interpretation counts — but they must be argued explicitly.
- **Mehta, arXiv:2607.09665 (FSI/PSI). MODERATE, but it lands a real hit.** Most measured "format
  sensitivity" is **answer-parse failure**, not reasoning failure. FI_in counts a paraphrase that
  merely breaks the answer extractor identically to a genuine reasoning failure. The project must state
  how parse failures are handled. (From the code: refusals are scored **incorrect, not dropped** —
  `main_body.tex:525` — which is the honest choice but concedes the confound.)
- **Nikitin et al., Kernel Language Entropy, arXiv:2405.20003. MODERATE.** H_sem is provably a special
  case of a strictly more general family, and KLE reports beating it. H_sem must be presented as *one
  instantiation* of answer dispersion, never as *the* canonical measure.
- **Gulliford et al. (2005), clinical epidemiology.** ICC on **binary** outcomes is mechanically coupled
  to outcome prevalence. **This predicts exactly the ρ_F–accuracy coupling (.26/.48/.41) the adversarial
  review recovered by imputation.** No NLP paper appears to know this. It is simultaneously the
  project's best opportunity (be the one to raise it) and an obligation (having found it, orthogonality
  cannot be claimed without addressing it).

### 3.5 Prior art on constructs the project treats as its own

- **Kobalczyk et al., "Active Task Disambiguation with LLMs", ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2502.04485).** Already
  derives a `log(|H|/n)` information-theoretic objective over a hypothesis set. **Do not claim novelty
  for the FI_spec functional form.**
- **Hou et al., "Decomposing Uncertainty … through Input Clarification Ensembling", ICML 2024,
  PMLR 235.** The ambiguous-versus-clarified contrast the project calls L0 vs L1 is **already published
  here, on AmbigQA, with semantic entropy as a baseline.** MODERATE–HIGH.
- **Dembski & Marks (2009), IEEE Trans. SMC-A 39(5):1051–1061.** Genuine peer-reviewed prior art for the
  −log₂(success probability) construction, as "endogenous information". Real, and carries
  intelligent-design association. Knowing it exists is necessary; citing it is optional.
- **Chen et al. (2021), pass@k.** The algebraic identity is real and should be conceded. The defence is
  that pass@k resamples *decodings of a fixed prompt* while FI_in resamples *question-side paraphrases*
  — different noise sources.
- **Keluskar et al., IEEE BigData 2024, pp. 7485–7490.** Confirms that feeding the human-disambiguated
  question is the standard oracle/ceiling condition. **The L1 condition is not a novel design.**
- **Razavi, Soltangheis, Arabzadeh, Salamat, Zihayat & Bagheri, "Benchmarking Prompt Sensitivity in
  Large Language Models", arXiv:2502.06065 / ECIR 2025** — independently confirmed by fetch, and the
  same paper I found unnamed in the project folder (§1.2). It owns the task name **Prompt Sensitivity
  Prediction** and shares an author with the QPP-GenRE line, so **the QPP-to-prompt-sensitivity bridge
  the project wants to build has already been built and published.**

### 3.6 What the QPP positioning can actually say

From **Meng et al. (ACM TOIS, DOI 10.1145/3736402)** over TREC DL 19–22 with lexical and neural
rankers: established QPP baselines span **Pearson −0.130 to 0.615 / Kendall −0.094 to 0.443** for
nDCG@10; their QPP-GenRE reaches Pearson 0.388–0.724 / Kendall 0.251–0.483. So state-of-the-art QPP
sits at roughly **Pearson 0.4–0.7, Kendall 0.25–0.5**, with individual baselines often near zero.

**Do not write "0.667 versus 0.4".** Three reasons: QPP reports rank/linear correlation against a
*continuous* per-query effectiveness score, whereas 0.667 is AUROC on a *binary* outcome — different
quantities; nearly all QPP numbers are in-distribution while 0.667 is out-of-distribution (which
favours the project, but must be said, not smuggled); and **Ganguly et al.** show QPP numbers move with
the choice of ground-truth effectiveness metric, so no crisp field baseline exists. The defensible
sentence is that both tasks are hard and that near-chance-to-moderate performance is the field norm.

### 3.7 Clean negative — the metric-collapse result appears genuinely unoccupied

Six differently-phrased searches plus six primary-source fetches found **no published study that
correlates several existing prompt-sensitivity indices to ask whether they measure one construct.**
Papers either propose a new index or compare evaluation *methodologies*.

Two near-misses that must be cited and distinguished: **Liu & Chu (arXiv:2604.18389)** correlate one
derived Cauchy–Schwarz bound against **one** metric (PromptSensiScore) — not a multi-index collapse
study; and **Qin et al. (ICML 2026)** criticise coarse output-comparison metrics but do not correlate
existing indices.

**This is the project's cleanest surviving contribution — and the window is closing.** Caveat on
completeness: the ProSA abs page and the construct-validity review (arXiv:2511.04703) were not fetched,
so this negative is weaker than a fetched positive.

## 5. The eight strands — what closed, and what is still open

### 5.1 Generalizability theory — **the apparatus is taken; the exact conjunction is not**

The paraphrase-facet-versus-resampling-facet split under explicit G-theory (G-study/D-study, REML,
Cronbach/Brennan/Shavelson cited) is published **twice in 2026**: Messing (arXiv:2604.11581) and
Żatuchin (arXiv:2607.13304). And **Cox et al. already define a per-question ratio of between-paraphrase
to total variance and call it the "prompt sensitivity ratio" ρ_u** — same symbol, same name, same
reading.

> ⚠️ One search agent reported Cox et al. as AAAI **2026**. The project's `references.bib` says AAAI-**25**,
> vol. 39, pp. 23696–23703, DOI `10.1609/aaai.v39i22.34540`. **The bib is right** — the DOI string
> `v39i22` fixes it to volume 39, which is AAAI-25. Do not "correct" it.

**What is genuinely still open** — stated first as the search strand concluded it, then **corrected in
§8.1, which removes one of the three legs**:

> ~~A variance share that is simultaneously **(a)** computed on gold-referenced **binary correctness**,
> **(b)** estimated **per question** rather than pooled, and **(c)** explicitly **noise-corrected** by
> subtracting MS_within in the ICC(1) sense.~~
>
> **Corrected (see §8.1): leg (c) is not novel.** The MS_within-subtracting estimator is textbook
> crossed-design ANOVA and has been standard in IR evaluation since 2007. **Only (a) and (b) survive.**

Seventeen queries across NLP, psychometric, G-theory, biostatistics and IR vocabulary found no paper
with all three. **Every near-miss drops exactly one leg:**

|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Cox et al., AAAI-25 (ρ_u) | ✗ embeddings | ✓ | ✗ compares to a 1/n_s baseline |
| Romanou et al., BrittleBench | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ deterministic decoding, no within-term exists |
| Mustahsan et al. (ICC(1)) | ✓ | ✗ pooled | ✓ |

**Present ρ_F as a recombination with a stated noise-correction property, not as a new construct.**

Also open, and worth knowing: **no paper applies G-theory or variance components to AmbigQA or to

- **Song, Lee & Jiao (2025), arXiv:2507.19980** — a full G-study (person × rater × task × dimension)
  plus D-study for LLM autoscoring.
- **Carlson & Burbano (2026), *Strategic Management Journal* 47(3):699–725** — proposes "Prompt Variance

**Unfetched but flagged:** Bowyer & Aitchison, arXiv:2503.01747, *"Don't Use the CLT in LLM Evals With
Fewer Than a Few Hundred Datapoints"* — directly relevant to the project's n = 149 error bars.

### 5.2 The AmbigQA protocol — **the project's deviation is unprecedented in its direction**

No published work pins **one** AmbigQA interpretation by a seeded rule and scores exclusively against
it at both levels. AmbigQA's native metric is set-F1 over all annotated pairs, and every major
follow-up preserves all-interpretation coverage (ASQA's Disambig-F1, ALCE, Tree of Clarifications,
CondAmbigQA — which explicitly penalises undergeneration).

**The crucial finding: the common LLM-era deviation runs the *opposite* way.** Cole et al. (EMNLP 2023)
and Staliūnaitė et al. (arXiv:2606.02093, June 2026) both use **max-over-all-references** — an
*any-match relaxation* — and Staliūnaitė et al. explicitly label it "common practice". **That is the
baseline a reviewer will have in mind, and it is more permissive than the project's, not less.**

The deviation is nonetheless *precedented*, just not *justified*, in prior work:
- **SituatedQA (EMNLP 2021)** pins a single extra-linguistic context per instance and scores single-answer EM.
- **Keluskar et al. (IEEE BigData 2024)** already evaluate AmbigQA as (ambiguous q, disambiguated q,
  single gold) triples — the closest structural precedent to the project's two-level ladder. Two limits
  found on fetching: they never justify departing from set-F1, and **they never state how the single
  gold is selected** when multiple disambiguations exist. **The project's seeded-hash rule is therefore
  more explicit than its closest prior art** — a real, if modest, differentiator.
- **Malaviya et al. (TACL 2025)** supply the citable published argument that evaluating responses to
  underspecified queries without fixed context is *"an ill-posed task"* (their word — not "invalid").

One caution recorded by the search agent and worth repeating: a low-quality extraction of the AmbigQA
PDF claimed a "single-reference variant" exists in the paper. The formula-bearing ar5iv read says
otherwise. **Treat the single-reference-variant claim as unsupported.**

### 5.3 Underspecification — **the anchor holds and the territory is nearly empty**

Kirchhof et al. (ICML 2025) verify exactly, and are stronger than assumed: they name underspecification
uncertainty as research direction #1, **explicitly cite Min et al. (AmbigQA) and the ~56 %-of-NQ-is-
ambiguous figure**, and call for research on how to detect and quantify it.

Of ~29 citing works, **exactly one** operationalises it: Matsnev (arXiv:2606.19559), and only as an
**LLM-elicited self-reported scalar** thresholded into a binary label, with no variance decomposition,
no paraphrase ensembles, no hidden states, no information-theoretic quantity, and no AmbigQA. It is a
single-author unrefereed preprint.

**So the FI / ρ_F / FI_spec stack is not scooped.** But the project must not claim to be *first to
answer Kirchhof's call*. The defensible narrowing: **first to answer it with an external, model-free
measurement model rather than model self-report.**

Two more results from this strand:
- **Hou et al. (ICML 2024) do NOT run the L0-vs-L1 contrast.** Their clarifications are LLM-generated
  and consumed *internally* as ensemble members; the ambiguous and disambiguated questions are never
  set up as two separate input conditions. **That contrast survives as novel.** But they do use question
  paraphrasing on NQ and GSM8K, so paraphrase ensembling over questions is not novel. Their AmbigQA
  ambiguity-detection AUROC is 71.7 (GPT) / 67.1 (Llama), with Semantic Entropy at 54.9 — **do not
  present the project's ~0.87 as a like-for-like win**, since it is a white-box probe against black-box
  ensembling on different splits.
- **Taparia et al., arXiv:2603.24967, "The Anatomy of Uncertainty in LLMs"** is the nearest published
  relative of the project's whole three-way framing (input ambiguity / knowledge gaps / decoding
  randomness). **It must be cited or a referee will find it.** Its structural weakness is the project's
  opening: they take **one greedy response per paraphrase**, so paraphrase-induced variance is
  confounded with residual generation variance and there is no within-cell mean square to subtract.
  ρ_F's noise correction is exactly that fix. **This is the single strongest "here is what we add"
  contrast available for the related-work section.**

### 5.4 The probes — **scooped three times over; only the linkage survives**

This is the strand that went worst for the project.

| work | venue | what it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| **Zhang et al., arXiv:2509.13664** | **EMNLP 2025 main** | ambiguity probing **on AmbigQA**, on **two of the project's three models**, 90.65–97.10 % in-domain accuracy, with **AmbigQA → SituatedQA transfer**. Prompt-based baselines 52–62 % |
| **Liu et al., arXiv:2601.17284** | **WWW 2026** | same architecture (linear), **same token position (last prompt token)**, same clear/ambiguous label semantics, two of the project's three models. AUROC 0.9891–0.9998 in-distribution |
| **Ramesh et al., arXiv:2606.05486** | preprint | third independent instance, within three months of writing |
| **Cencerrado et al., arXiv:2509.10625** | ICLR 2026 workshop | question-only linear probes predicting **answer correctness**, OOD transfer, 7B–70B |
| **Kossen et al., arXiv:2406.15927** | **ICML 2024 *workshop*** | probes predicting **semantic entropy** at **TBG** — the project's exact token position |

**"A linear probe on hidden states predicts question ambiguity" is dead as a novelty claim**, as is
cross-dataset transfer of such a probe.

Two corrections to defences that were being contemplated:
1. **Token position is not a differentiator.** Kossen's TBG *is* the last prompt token, which is what
   `dump_hidden_states.py` uses. Liu et al. (WWW 2026) use it too.
2. **Continuous-target regression is only half a differentiator.** Kossen et al.'s **Appendix A.12
   already tried soft labelling** and reported it did not improve performance. So a genuine regression
   onto continuous H_sem is territory they entered and abandoned, not virgin ground.

**What actually survives:** none of these papers connects the ambiguity signal to answer-side
quantities. Zhang et al. explicitly do **not** relate ambiguity to answer entropy, dispersion, or
correctness. Ramesh et al. make no such connection either. **The project's differentiator must be the
linkage — probe → dispersion, reliability, and the FI/ρ_F measurement model — not the probe.**
Two further advantages worth stating: the project uses **AmbigQA's human-annotated interpretations**,
where Ramesh et al. use LLM-synthesised ambiguity with a **12-prompt** human gold set; and the
project's OOD drop is the more informative number, since Liu et al.'s 0.99 in-distribution alongside
the project's 0.667 OOD is exactly the generalisation story worth telling.

Also: **Staliūnaitė et al. (arXiv:2606.02093)** already show ambiguity information improves downstream
error prediction by **>10 PRR points**, so "ambiguity information helps" is not a first demonstration.

### 5.5 The paraphrase instrument — **one worry to drop, two to state harder**

**Drop the diversity-collapse hedge.** The only direct head-to-head measurement runs the *other* way:
**Cegin et al., EMNLP 2023 main, pp. 1889–1905** find ChatGPT paraphrases **more** lexically
(+28.75 % unique words) and syntactically (tree edit distance 19.0 vs 13.7, p = 0.001) diverse than
human crowdworkers. The homogenization literature concerns free-form generation, not constrained
paraphrasing of a fixed seed. *(Note: this is the EMNLP 2023 paper, **not** Cegin et al. ACL 2024 —
see §3.1.)*

**State harder #1 — FI_in is a lower bound.** BrittleBench verifies exactly as reported: paraphrasing
is the **weakest** of their four perturbation families (0.13–5.01 % drops, sometimes *improving*
open-weight models, versus up to 12.83 % for word manipulation and padding), and they warn LLM
paraphrasing "might make queries easier … by standardizing language and decreasing diversity". The
project measures sensitivity **with the mildest available probe**. Say so — it makes the effects
conservative, which is a strength.

**State harder #2 — the NLI gate is the weaker link, and there is now a proper citation for it.**
**Jiang & de Marneffe (TACL 2022)** name **"Interrogative Hypothesis"** as a category in their
NLI-disagreement taxonomy and state that **questions lack truth values, so the entailment label is
theoretically ill-defined for them.** The project applies a 0.9 threshold to precisely this ill-defined
quantity. **This citation is much stronger than Ben Abacha & Demner-Fushman**, whose supporting numbers
(74.9 % RQE vs 98 % NLI) are medical-domain, 2019-era, and confounded. No one has published
"DeBERTa-MNLI achieves X % on question paraphrase pairs" — **so state the limitation as
ill-definedness plus an unquantified error rate, and do not import 74.9 % as if it were the filter's
accuracy.**

**Generator-relativity is a shared, acknowledged open problem, not a unique oversight.** No published
paraphraser-swap ablation exists for any prompt-sensitivity metric; PTEB (arXiv:2510.06730v3) lists it
as unresolved future work in its own limitations. So a single Qwen- or Mistral-paraphrased replication
of one condition would be **a real contribution, not merely a robustness check** — worth reconsidering
the decision to drop R6.

### 5.6 Functional information outside biology — **the narrow claim survives**

**No work of any kind applies Szostak/Hazen functional information, or −log₂(surviving fraction), to
LLM prompts, paraphrases, prompt sensitivity, question ambiguity, or in-context learning.** Confirmed
via Semantic Scholar citation-graph queries on both anchor papers (the arXiv API indexes only
title/abstract/author and is useless for this).

But the surrounding context is less empty than assumed:
- **Two independent September 2025 arXiv preprints export the identical formalism into quantum
  physics** — one to **random quantum circuits**, which is a discrete, sampled, threshold-scored
  configuration space *structurally very close to the paraphrase space*; one to Quantum Darwinism.
- A CS-categorised **"active information"** literature (Díaz-Pachón et al.) cites Szostak 2003 and
  Hazen 2007 as foundations while sitting squarely in the **Dembski/Marks/Ewert evolutionary-informatics
  line**. Combined with Dembski & Marks (2009) defining −log₂(p) as "endogenous information" in a real
  IEEE journal, this is a **reputational adjacency worth knowing about before a public talk**.

**On the Wong et al. (2023) law: "disputed" is the correct word.** The complete critical record is
**exactly one PNAS Letter (Root-Bernstein 2024, 121(34) e2318689121) plus the same-issue Reply
(e2406598121)**, both verified via Crossref, PubMed and Europe PMC. No second Letter, Comment or
Erratum exists. No peer-reviewed philosophy-of-science rejection exists. No independent formalisation,
replication, or empirical validation by anyone outside the Wong/Hazen group exists. The sustained
hostile critiques are non-peer-reviewed (Zenil's Medium post; an intelligent-design-adjacent outlet).
**Root-Bernstein's letter attacks evolutionary directionality and teleology, not the mathematics of FI
as a measure** — so it does not bear on FI_in as a measurement device, and citing it as evidence the
law is contested is accurate and safe.

⚠️ One negative result is itself a finding about method: a web-search summariser **asserted that FI has
been applied to materials discovery and produced zero supporting sources.** That is a hallucination,
recorded here so it is not re-introduced.

### 5.7 Sensitivity versus ability — **the orthogonality claim is the most exposed thing in the project**

The literature is split **systematically by unit of analysis**:

| unit | what the literature reports |
|---|---|
| across **prompts** | **strong negative coupling.** Lu et al. (NAACL 2024) report **r = −0.8764, p ≪ 0.01**, and explicitly propose sensitivity as an *unsupervised proxy for accuracy* |
| across **models/sizes** | weak and contradictory. POSIX states in print that "accuracy and sensitivity are two separate aspects"; Sclar et al. find sensitivity survives scaling, few-shot and instruction tuning; ProSA finds the opposite headline (larger models more robust) |
| across **questions** — the project's actual unit | **no published correlation exists at all** |

So: the axis-separation claim is **defensible as a conceptual/design claim with a direct citation
(POSIX)**, and the per-question measurement is **genuine residual novelty**. But three things make it
the weakest ground in the project:

1. **The strongest published measurement contradicts it** (Lu et al., r = −0.88), albeit at a different
   unit of analysis. That distinction must be made explicitly, not left implicit.
2. **There is a known mechanical confound, and it is not in the NLP literature at all.**
   **Gulliford et al. (2005)**, clinical epidemiology: **ICC on binary outcomes is mechanically coupled
   to outcome prevalence.** This predicts precisely the ρ_F–accuracy coupling (.26/.48/.41) the
   adversarial review recovered by imputation. Having found it, the project **cannot claim orthogonality
   without addressing it** — but it can be the paper that raises it in NLP, which is a contribution.
3. **The single most favourable lead has evaporated.** The anonymous "Prompt Gallery" submission in the
   project folder — the one reporting that sensitivity does not correlate with model size or baseline
   metrics — **has no published version.** Four targeted queries found no arXiv entry, no ACL
   Anthology entry, no OpenReview forum; the term "Prompt Gallery" never appeared. **It is uncitable.**

And a fourth: **no paper reports a near-zero sensitivity–accuracy correlation.** Papers arguing for
separation do so by counterexample, design, or framing — never by reporting r ≈ 0. **Do not expect a
published precedent for an orthogonality number.**

### 5.8 QPP and the 0.667 — **a much better comparator than the one being sought**

The ECIR 2025 paper is confirmed: **Razavi, Soltangheis, Arabzadeh, Salamat, Zihayat & Bagheri,
"Benchmarking Prompt Sensitivity in Large Language Models", ECIR 2025, LNCS 15574, pp. 303–313,
DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-88714-7_29** (arXiv:2502.06065).

Three results:
1. **It scoops the task framing but not the probes.** It defines "predict from a prompt whether an LLM
   will answer it correctly" and benchmarks it — **but only with surface-text and text-embedding
   methods. It never touches hidden states.** So the project's probes are not scooped by it; the *task
   name and framing* are.
2. **Exactly one paper has improved on its baselines:** **VAP3 (Arabzadeh & Bagheri, SIGIR 2025,
   pp. 2794–2799)**, same group, reaching **0.693 / 0.730** accuracy against the ECIR paper's
   0.660 / 0.710.
3. **The embedding-specificity ancestor is real but not an exact match.** The
   Arabzadeh/Zarrinkalam/Bagheri line (CIKM 2019, ECIR 2020, IPM 57(4) 2020) operationalises specificity
   as **local neighbourhood density** — ego networks, clusters — **not as distance to a designated
   most-specific point x\***. So the supervisor's proposal has an **established structural ancestor but
   no exact predecessor**, which is a good outcome: cite the ancestry, keep the contribution.

**The right comparator for AUROC 0.667 is not the QPP correlation literature.** QPP reports Kendall
τ ≈ 0.1–0.4 against average precision — a different quantity on a different outcome type, and
**Ganguly et al.** show those numbers move with the choice of ground-truth metric, so no crisp field
baseline exists. **Do not write "0.667 versus 0.4".**

**Use this instead:** *"No Answer Needed"* (Cencerrado et al., ICLR 2026 workshop) reports AUROC for
**last-prompt-token linear probes**, **out of distribution**, on **the project's exact three model
families**: **0.53–0.88**. **0.667 sits mid-band, and saying so is both honest and favourable.**

### 6.3 🆕 Genuinely novel — the surviving contribution

Ordered by how well each survived the sweep.

1. **The analytic metric-collapse result.** **No published study correlates several existing
   prompt-sensitivity indices to ask whether they measure one construct**, and no one gives the exact
   reduction. Two qualifications, both manageable:
   - **Chen et al. (ICML 2026 Position Track)** argue the same thesis *in prose* ("mechanically
     isomorphic to an unsupervised clustering problem") but give **no formal reduction**, and never
     touch prompt-sensitivity indices (no S_τ, no POSIX, no TVD, no Errica). **Cite it and sharpen the
     claim to "exact functional identity, previously argued only informally."**
   - **Their Table 1 is a direct empirical counterweight:** published UQ indices agree at only
     **Jaccard 0.08–0.40** on which items are most uncertain. So the project must claim
     *identity-in-formula*, and **explain why that coexists with disagreement-in-ranking** — which it
     does, because the indices are different functions of the same sufficient statistic.
   - **Nikitin et al. (KLE, NeurIPS 2024)** already prove semantic entropy is a special case of a
     richer metric, so *deriving exact relations between UQ metrics* is established practice. **The
     project's novelty is breadth and direction:** KLE generalises *upward* to motivate a **new**
     metric; the project collapses *downward* across five heterogeneous indices from different
     literatures onto one sufficient statistic, to motivate **fewer**. Say that explicitly.
   - ⚠️ **The window is closing.** Liu & Chu (ACL 2026) already correlate a derived bound against one
     metric. Priority should be high.
2. **ρ_F as the (a)+(b) recombination** — gold-referenced binary correctness × per-question estimation,
   using a standard noise-corrected estimator. ⚠️ **Revised down by §8.1:** the MS_within subtraction is
   *not* novel (Urbano et al., SIGIR 2013, eqs. 1–3; Bodoff & Li, SIGIR 2007). Present ρ_F as
   *"a G-theory apparatus IR evaluation has used since 2007, transplanted onto the paraphrase facet
   with a gold-referenced binary outcome, estimated per question"* — still a contribution, a smaller
   one than the strands implied, and stating it correctly is cheap insurance against an IR reviewer.
3. **The L0-vs-L1 contrast as two separate input conditions with matched downstream measurement.**
   Hou et al. (ICML 2024) consume clarifications *internally* as ensemble members and never set up the
   two conditions separately. **Verified clean negative.**
4. **G-theory / variance components applied to AmbigQA and to annotated question ambiguity.** No
   antecedent found. FI_spec has no variance-components predecessor.
5. **The linkage from an ambiguity probe to answer-side quantities** (dispersion, reliability, and the
   FI/ρ_F model). The probes themselves are scooped; **none of the scooping papers makes this
   connection** — Zhang et al. explicitly do not.
6. **The seeded-hash target-selection rule**, which is more explicit than Keluskar et al., the closest
   prior art, who never state how their single gold is chosen.
7. **Raising the binary-ICC prevalence confound (Gulliford et al. 2005) in NLP.** No NLP paper appears
   to know it. This is an obligation *and* an opportunity.
8. **A paraphraser-swap ablation, if it were run.** No published prompt-sensitivity metric has one, and
   PTEB lists it as open. **This reframes R6 from "nice to have" to "a real contribution" —
   worth reconsidering the decision to drop it.**

## 8. Completeness audit — what eight strands and ~140 queries still missed

A final adversarial pass asked what vocabulary was never tried and what neighbouring literature nobody
looked at, then ran those searches. It changed several conclusions above. **These are the most
important findings in this document**, because they come from fields the project has not been reading.

### 8.1 ⛔ The MS_within leg of ρ_F's novelty is falsified

**Urbano, Marrero & Martín, "On the Measurement of Test Collection Reliability", SIGIR 2013,
pp. 393–402, DOI 10.1145/2484028.2484038** — read directly from the author-hosted PDF.

It runs an explicit G-study/D-study on **43 TREC collections** with a fully crossed design decomposing
score variance into systems, query difficulty, and their interaction. **Its equations (1)–(3) are
precisely the MS_within-subtracting estimator:**

```
sigma^2_e = EM_residual
sigma^2_s = (EM_s - sigma^2_e) / n_q
sigma^2_q = (EM_q - sigma^2_e) / n_s
```

And the apparatus entered IR evaluation earlier still: **Bodoff & Li, "Test theory for assessing IR
test collections", SIGIR 2007** (extended in *IP&M* 44(3):1117–1145, 2008), proposed generalizability
theory as the principled alternative to swap-rate and Kendall-τ heuristics, on the explicit analogy
*"testing algorithms is like testing students"* — **the same psychometric move the project makes with
questions and paraphrases.**

**Consequence:** "explicit MS_within subtraction" is not a leg of ρ_F's novelty. It is the standard
ANOVA variance-component estimator, in continuous use in IR evaluation for nineteen years.

**And G-theory is not new to NLP either.** **Bayerl & Paul, "Identifying Sources of Disagreement:
Generalizability Theory in Manual Annotation Studies", *Computational Linguistics* 33(1):3–8, 2007** —
in NLP's flagship journal, decomposing annotator disagreement into facets. Any sentence framing
G-theory as an import that is new to NLP is false, and an ACL-community reviewer is likely to know it.
*(Bibliographic record confirmed via OpenAlex; the body could not be fetched — MIT Press returns 403.
Read it before characterising its facet structure in detail.)*

### 8.2 ⛔ The most serious methodological risk in the project, and nobody had found it

Urbano et al. §3 demonstrates **empirically** that variance-component estimates are **wildly unstable
when estimated from few units**. Their reported findings: with 30 initial queries, G-theory suggests
anywhere from **63 to 133** queries to hit the same reliability target; estimates *"can vary in orders
of magnitude if not using enough data"*; and **"at least 50 queries and 50 systems seem necessary for
95 % of estimates to be within a 0.1 span."**

**The project estimates a variance ratio *per question* from a 10-paraphrase × 10-sample grid.** That
is far below the regime Urbano et al. show is needed for stability — and it comes with a published
empirical demonstration, not a theoretical worry.

This bears directly on the review's finding that ρ_F's bootstrap CI median width is 0.18 and that the
Δρ_F tests are underpowered. **Those are not separate problems; they are this problem.** Before any
per-question ρ_F value is interpreted, its sampling variability has to be quantified.

**The tool is in the same paper.** Urbano §2.3, equations (8)–(11), gives **Feldt's exact confidence
interval for a variance ratio** ζ = σ²_s/σ²_e mapped onto the generalizability coefficient, plus
Arteaga et al.'s CI for the dependability index, with Brennan cited on robustness under non-normality.
**This is directly transplantable machinery for reporting ρ_F with error bars, which the project
currently lacks.** It is the single highest-value item in this document for the R2 work.

Pair it with two more: **Messing** reports naive standard errors are **40–60 % smaller** than
error-corrected ones, and **Bowyer & Aitchison (arXiv:2503.01747)** find CLT-based intervals
*"dramatically underestimate uncertainty"* below a few hundred datapoints. **Together those make a
two-citation case that at n = 149 the project must not use CLT intervals.**

### 8.3 ⛔ The strongest technical attack on FI — and it is peer-reviewed philosophy, not a blog

§5.6 audited the critical record on Wong et al.'s *biological* claim. **Nobody audited the critical
record on the *formalism*.**

FI as the project uses it — fix a threshold, count the configurations that clear it, take −log₂ of the
fraction — shares its skeleton with Dembski's **specified complexity**. The canonical objection to that
family is that **the number is an artefact of the probability measure imposed on the configuration
space, which is chosen rather than discovered.** It is published twice, in serious venues:

- **Fitelson, Stephens & Sober, "How Not to Detect Design", *Philosophy of Science* 66(3), 1999**,
  DOI 10.1086/392699.
- **Elsberry & Shallit, "Information theory, evolutionary computation, and Dembski's 'complex specified
  information'", *Synthese* 178(2):237–270, 2009/2011**, DOI 10.1007/s11229-009-9542-8.

**This transfers to the project with more force than to its original target**, because FI_in's
denominator is the empirical distribution of **one generator's paraphrases** — Phi-4, 8 personas,
T = 0.8 — an explicitly arbitrary and swappable reference class. Combined with §5.5's finding that
**no paraphraser-swap ablation exists anywhere in the literature**, this is the sharpest available
attack on FI_in.

Three consequences:
1. **Pre-empt it in the limitations rather than be shown it.** The Corona et al. finite-U formulation
   (§1.3) is the constructive answer: state the reference class, do not pretend it is the space.
2. This supersedes Zenil's Medium post as the citation for observer-relativity — **same argument, peer
   reviewed, and twenty-five years older.**
3. It is also a **reputational caution**: the nearest CS-side neighbour of this formalism (the
   Díaz-Pachón "active information" line, §5.6) sits in exactly the intellectual lineage the *Synthese*
   paper is refuting.

### 8.4 Survey methodology — the project's core assumption has a name, and it is known to fail

Eight strands ran ~140 queries across NLP, IR, psychometrics, biostatistics and philosophy. **Not one
used survey-research vocabulary** — even though *"same question, different phrasing, different answer"*
is the founding observation of that field, with a fifty-year quantitative record in *Public Opinion
Quarterly*.

**Huber & Paris, "Assessing the Programmatic Equivalence Assumption in Question Wording Experiments",
*Public Opinion Quarterly* 77(S1), 2013**, DOI 10.1093/poq/nfs054.

The **"programmatic equivalence assumption"** is the premise that two wordings of a question refer to
the same underlying object and differ only in framing. **That is exactly what the project assumes when
it treats 10 Phi-4 paraphrases plus an NLI filter as meaning-preserving variants of one question.**
Huber & Paris show experimentally that it *fails*: "welfare" and "assistance to the poor" are not
programmatically equivalent — respondents conceptualise different objects, so the measured wording
effect **confounds framing with referent shift**.

This is a far better-grounded statement of the instrument-validity worry than anything in the NLP
literature, it is peer-reviewed and thirteen years old, and it lets the project report the NLI-filter
limitation as **a known measurement problem with an established name** rather than as a hand-wave. The
surrounding canon also supplies useful framing: phrasing sensitivity is a property of questions and
respondents **generally**, not a defect peculiar to LLMs.

### 8.5 ρ_F already has a name in statistics

ρ_F is, in standard terms, a **first-order variance-based global sensitivity index — a Sobol index over
the paraphrase factor.** Sobol indices are already used in ML interpretability (**Fel et al., "Look at
the Variance! Efficient Black-box Explanations with Sobol-based Sensitivity Analysis", NeurIPS,
arXiv:2111.04138**), applied to image regions and tokens.

A date-sorted arXiv sweep on Sobol + language models returned **nothing applying variance-based global
sensitivity analysis to prompt paraphrase space**, so the *application* is genuinely open. But the
*quantity* has a name. **One sentence positioning ρ_F as a first-order Sobol index estimated with a
noise correction costs nothing and immunises against "you reinvented sensitivity analysis."**

### 8.6 Human label variation — the missing NLP-native bridge

**Plank, "The 'Problem' of Human Label Variation: On Ground Truth in Data, Modeling and Evaluation",
EMNLP 2022** (93 citations), and **Weber-Genzel et al., "VariErr NLI: Separating Annotation Error from
Human Label Variation", ACL 2024**.

This is the NLP-native argument that **disagreement driven by genuine item ambiguity is signal, not
noise** — the conceptual bridge between the AmbigQA specificity dial and mainstream NLP. It licenses
treating irreducible answer variation on ambiguous items as a property of the item, which is what
FI_spec assumes. And **VariErr NLI performs structurally the same separation the project performs** —
genuine variation versus error — in the annotation domain. It is the natural analogy for defending the
paraphrase-variance/sampling-noise split as a familiar move rather than an exotic one.

### 8.7 Two claims above are weakened

- **The probe-linkage novelty (§6.3 item 5) is narrower than stated.** **Yang, Li, Piedrahita Velez,
  Todd, Bau, Littman, Bach & Pavlick, "Shared Lexical Task Representations Explain Behavioral
  Variability in LLMs", ICML 2026, arXiv:2604.22027** — from a top group, found by no strand. They
  identify "lexical task heads" and show **behavioural variation between prompts is explained by how
  strongly those heads activate**, attributing failures to competing task representations. That is the
  mechanistic version of predicting dispersion from representations. **The project's specific target —
  a calibrated per-question variance share grounded in ambiguity annotations — survives, but restate
  the contribution as that, not as "first to link internals to prompt-induced variability."**
- **Cox et al. concede less than §5.1 implied, which helps.** Verified at full-text level: ρ_u is
  computed on the **trace of the embedding covariance matrix**, with correctness entering only
  downstream as the binary target the metric is asked to predict. So Cox et al. occupy *"per-question
  between-paraphrase variance share of embedding dispersion"*, **not** *"per-question noise-corrected
  paraphrase variance share of binary correctness."* Use the narrower statement — the broader one
  concedes more than the evidence requires. **But the name-and-symbol collision (ρ_u vs ρ_F, both
  called a prompt sensitivity ratio, both per question) is severe enough that the project should either
  rename ρ_F or distinguish it from ρ_u in the same paragraph that introduces the symbol.**
