# Improve Scientific Writing

Make the scientific argument easier to understand without changing the science. Optimize for accurate communication: readers should recover the intended question, logic, evidence, scope, and emphasis with minimal unnecessary effort.

## Compose with specialized skills

Use this skill as the general writing layer. When the paper targets a named conference, journal, discipline, section, or artifact type, also use the applicable specialized skill. The specialized skill governs venue requirements, disciplinary conventions, required sections, review criteria, and formatting; this skill continues to govern general clarity and argument flow. If guidance conflicts, follow the user's instructions and authoritative current requirements first, then the specialized skill, rather than forcing this generic guidance.

## Protect scientific integrity

- Never invent or silently strengthen claims, evidence, results, citations, methods, novelty, limitations, or causal interpretations.
- Preserve intended meaning, notation, terminology, citations, labels, and voice unless the user asks to change them.
- Mark missing support, unclear logic, and scientific ambiguity as author decisions or evidence gaps. Do not solve them through confident prose.
- Distinguish observation, analysis, interpretation, and speculation. Match certainty and scope to the underlying evidence.

## Revision workflow

1. Identify whether the request is diagnosis, drafting, structural revision, line editing, compression, or audit. Stay within that scope.
2. Recover the argument: research question, relevant gap, central claims, evidence for each claim, limitations, and why the result matters. Resolve argument-level problems before polishing sentences.
3. Give every section and paragraph one discernible job. Arrange material so context precedes new information and each step prepares the next.
4. Revise for reader expectations: keep subjects near their verbs; express important actions in verbs; begin sentences with the continuing topic or needed context; reserve syntactic closure for information that deserves emphasis. Treat these as diagnostic principles, not rigid grammar rules.
5. Use stable terms for stable concepts, define abbreviations before use where needed, replace vague references, and prefer concrete claims over announcements of author activity.
6. Check that citations support the exact nearby claim and that figures, tables, and captions can be interpreted without guesswork. Do not impose a citation style, English variety, tense scheme, or LaTeX package unless the user or target venue requires it.
7. Re-read for coherence and evidential fidelity. When editing a long draft, separate structural, paragraph, and sentence passes so local polish does not conceal a broken argument.

For detailed diagnostics, examples, and source-derived checks, read [references/scientific-writing-principles.md](references/scientific-writing-principles.md).

## Output contract

Return the requested revised text or diagnosis first. Then briefly identify material changes and any unresolved evidence gaps, ambiguities, or venue-dependent choices. For a focused edit, do not expand the response into a whole-paper review.
